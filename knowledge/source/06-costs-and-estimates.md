# Costs, payments and estimates

## Factor rates and interest rates
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/resources/factor-rate-vs-interest-rate/

A factor rate is a decimal multiplier, typically between 1.1 and 1.5, applied to the amount funded to set the total repayment upfront. Example: $10,000 at a 1.2 factor rate means $12,000 is repaid. Factor rates are used for short-term financing such as revenue-based financing and some bridge financing; payments are usually daily or weekly, and repaying early does not lower the fixed total. An interest rate is a percentage that accrues over time on the outstanding balance and is usually quoted annually (APR); it is used for term loans, lines of credit and SBA financing, and early repayment saves interest. Factor-rate financing is generally more expensive but faster and easier to qualify for.

## Payment versus total cost
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/sbgfunding-calculator/

The payment is what leaves your account each period. The total cost is everything you repay over the whole term, including fees, minus the amount you received. Comparing offers on payment alone can be misleading, because a longer term lowers the payment while raising the total cost. SBG's calculator shows both for that reason.

## Fees at a glance
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/

- Upfront fees: none on SBG's core products.
- Application: free, no obligation, soft credit pull only.
- Line of credit: a draw fee of up to 2% per withdrawal; no maintenance, annual or inactivity fees.
- Invoice financing: 0.5% to 5% per month on the advanced amount plus 0% to 2% origination.
- Prepayment: many products have no prepayment penalty, and some offer prepayment discounts or interest forgiveness; your agreement spells out the specifics.
- Term loans: rates start at 1.25% per month and vary with the business profile.
- SBA 7(a): rates tied to the prime rate with SBA-mandated caps.

## How SBG's funding calculator estimates an offer
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/sbgfunding-calculator/

SBG's public funding calculator gives an instant estimate of the funding amount, payment and total cost from three inputs: time in business, credit score and monthly revenue. Each input earns points, the total places the profile in a pricing tier, and the tier sets a revenue multiplier, a rate factor, a term and a payment frequency. Higher tiers allow a larger multiple of monthly revenue, longer terms (12 to 36 months) and less frequent payments (weekly, twice monthly or monthly). Profiles below the lowest tier do not get an automatic estimate; the calculator then suggests other options such as a HELOC and talking with a funding advisor. If you request less than the maximum, the estimate shows payments for your requested amount.

The estimate is a model, not an offer. A real offer comes from reviewing your 4 most recent business bank statements against more than 50 data points. An estimate cannot see a signed contract, a seasonal pattern or a strong month that has not landed yet; if the figure looks low, that is usually what is missing, and a funding advisor can size the offer against what the model could not read.

## How SBG's line of credit calculator works
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/line-of-credit-calculator/

The line of credit calculator estimates a credit limit, a monthly payment and a monthly rate from monthly revenue. The limit is what you can draw, not what you owe: a $100,000 limit with nothing drawn costs nothing, and the monthly rate applies only to the drawn balance. The payment moves with the balance - draw more and it rises, repay and it falls. Lines of credit run from $5,000 to $500,000; for larger amounts a term loan is the right product.

## Collateral, UCC filings and personal guarantees
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/no-collateral-business-loans/

Collateral is a specific asset pledged to secure financing. SBG's unsecured products (term loans, lines of credit, working capital, bridge capital and revenue-based financing) require none. A UCC filing is a public notice that a lender holds a security interest in business assets; it is standard in business financing and does not attach to personal property such as your home or car. A personal guarantee means the owner agrees to be personally responsible if the business cannot repay; whether one applies depends on the product and amount, and your advisor explains the terms before you sign.

## How much can I borrow?
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/faqs/

SBG offers financing from $5,000 to $10 million depending on the product and qualifications. Lines of credit go up to $500,000 and SBA financing up to $5 million; equipment financing covers up to 90% of the equipment's value and invoice financing up to 90% of invoice value. The approved amount is driven mainly by monthly revenue, deposit consistency, time in business and the overall profile. Larger amounts are available through SBG's financing marketplace.

## Do you offer monthly payments?
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/faqs/

Yes. Monthly repayment options are available for qualifying businesses, and the structure depends on the product. Term loans and working capital offer weekly or monthly payments; revenue-based financing is repaid through automatic daily or weekly debits.
