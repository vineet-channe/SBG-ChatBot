# Industries SBG serves

## How industry affects financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/industries/

SBG structures financing around how each sector's cash flow actually works rather than a generic credit box. It finances most industries; adult entertainment and gambling are excluded. The sections below list the products SBG recommends most often for each sector and what each is typically used for. Businesses outside these sectors (for example fitness, telecommunications, home technology, agriculture or professional services) are also funded.

## Automotive services financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/industries/automotive-services/

Typical challenge: The bays are full. The parts order is overdue. Payroll is Monday.

Recommended products:

- Business Term Loan: Adding a service bay, shop expansion, a second location. Term: 6-48 months; minimum FICO 500.
- Business Line of Credit: Parts inventory, payroll, day-to-day operating costs. Term: Revolving; minimum FICO 500.
- Equipment Financing: Lifts, diagnostic tools, tire equipment, paint booths. Term: 2-5 years; minimum FICO 500.
- Invoice Financing: Unlocking cash from fleet accounts and commercial clients. Term: Per invoice; approval based on your customers' credit.
- Working Capital: One large parts order or slow stretch, with a fixed payoff date. Term: 3-24 months; minimum FICO 500.

Common uses:

- Shop Equipment and Lifts: Two-post lifts, alignment racks, diagnostic scanners, and tire machines are essential, and expensive.
- Parts and Inventory: Stocking the right parts means faster turnaround and more completed jobs per day.
- Technician Payroll: Skilled technicians are your most valuable resource and the hardest to replace.
- Paint Booth and Body Shop Equipment: Adding collision repair capability significantly expands your revenue potential.
- Fleet and Commercial Account Gaps: Fleet accounts and commercial clients provide steady volume but typically pay on net-30 terms.
- Shop Expansion or Relocation: Growing into a larger facility or opening a second location requires lease deposits, build-out costs, and equipment, all before new revenue arrives.

- Business Expansion: Growing in construction means capital for additional crews, fleet vehicles, upgraded equipment, or the runway to bid larger projects.
