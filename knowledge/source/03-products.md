## Revenue-based financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/resources/unlock-business-potential-with-mca-loans/

Revenue-based financing (RBF) is SBG Funding's core direct product. The business receives a lump sum today in exchange for a share of its future sales, and repays an agreed total through small daily or weekly payments that are automatically debited from the business bank account. It is not a loan: there is no interest rate, and the total repayment is fixed when you sign.

- Sizing: based on average monthly deposits and how steady they are, read from the 4 most recent business bank statements.
- Cost: set with a factor rate, a multiplier on the amount funded. Example from SBG: a $50,000 advance at a 1.3 factor rate means $65,000 is repaid in total, and that number does not change.
- Term: no set term; repayment flexes with cash flow - more when sales are strong, less when they are slow.
- Requirements: FICO 500+, 6+ months in business and $150,000+ annual revenue. No collateral and no hard credit pull.
- Best for: urgent needs, uneven or seasonal revenue, limited collateral or credit history, and purchases that pay for themselves quickly (seasonal inventory, a marketing push with a measurable return, equipment that adds capacity right away).
- Trade-off: RBF is usually more expensive than a term loan or line of credit, so it works best for needs with a clear, near-term return. For ongoing needs a line of credit may fit better; for large planned investments compare a term loan or SBA 7(a).

## Small business term loan
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/small-business-term-loan/

A term loan provides a lump sum upfront that is repaid on a fixed schedule over a set term. It is best for a specific investment with a defined cost.

- Amount: $5,000 to $10,000,000.
- Term: 6 to 48 months; shorter 6 to 12 month terms are available for a defined payoff timeline.
- Payments: fixed weekly or monthly payments.
- Rates: starting at 1.25% per month; rates vary with the business profile.
- Collateral: none; term loans are unsecured.
- Fees: no upfront fees and no prepayment penalty; some programs offer early payoff discounts.
- Speed: same-day decisions; funds in as little as 24 hours after approval and signed documents.
- Best for: renovations, expansion, a second location, mobilizing on a new contract, a large tooling or inventory buy.

## Business line of credit
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/business-line-of-credit/

A business line of credit is revolving: draw what you need, repay it, and draw again. Interest is charged only on the outstanding drawn balance, never on the unused limit.

- Credit limit: $5,000 to $500,000. For amounts above $500,000 a term loan is the right product.
- Structure: revolving with no fixed end date; draws are deposited the same day once the line is active.
- Payments: weekly or monthly; the payment tracks what you owe and falls as you repay.
- Fees: a draw fee of up to 2% of each withdrawal; no maintenance, annual or inactivity fees.
- Collateral: none; the line is unsecured.
- Requirements: FICO 500+, 6+ months in business, $150,000+ annual revenue, 4 bank statements.
- Credit building: account activity is reported to business credit bureaus once the line is active.
- Limit increases: may be requested as the business grows and shows consistent repayment.
- Best for: payroll, inventory, bridging invoice gaps, equipment repairs and other recurring or unpredictable needs.

## Working capital financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/working-capital-loans/

Working capital financing is a fixed amount for one defined operating gap, sized at approval and repaid on a set schedule.

- Amount: $5,000 to $10,000,000.
- Term: 3 to 24 months with weekly or monthly payments.
- Use: any legitimate operating expense - payroll, inventory, rent, utilities, suppliers, marketing, repairs or taxes. You are not required to specify how funds will be spent.
- Collateral: none; working capital is unsecured.
- Seasonal businesses: SBG evaluates full annual revenue history rather than a single month, so a strong peak season counts.
- Versus a line of credit: choose working capital for a one-time, defined amount with a fixed payoff; choose a line of credit for recurring or variable needs.

## SBA 7(a) financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/sba-loans/

The SBA 7(a) program is the U.S. Small Business Administration's primary lending program. The SBA does not lend directly; it guarantees part of financing made by approved lenders, which allows larger amounts, longer terms and lower rates than conventional financing. SBG arranges SBA financing through its lending partners and guides the application.

- Amount: up to $5,000,000.
- Term: 10 to 25 years.
- Rates: tied to the prime rate and subject to SBA-mandated maximums that vary by size and term.
- Collateral: may be required depending on size and purpose. Financing under $50,000 typically does not require collateral; for larger amounts the SBA requires lenders to take available collateral, but insufficient collateral alone does not disqualify a strong application.
- Credit: SBG accepts applicants from 500, but SBA approval also applies SBA criteria. Scores of 650 and above are generally stronger candidates.
- Timeline: typically 30 to 90 days from application to funding - much slower than SBG's conventional products. If the need is urgent, a term loan may be the better fit.
- Eligibility: the business must operate for profit in the U.S., meet the SBA size standard for its industry, have reasonable owner equity, have exhausted other financing options and not be in an ineligible industry (such as gambling and certain financial businesses).
- Documents: start with the application and 4 bank statements; SBG then provides the full SBA checklist (tax returns, P&L, SBA forms, purpose documents).
- Best for: business acquisitions, large build-outs, buying real estate or a second location, and situations where long-term cost matters more than speed.

## Equipment financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/equipment-financing/

Equipment financing funds machinery, vehicles, technology and other physical business assets. The equipment itself serves as collateral, and you own it outright from day one.

- Amount: up to 90% of the equipment's value (loan-to-value).
- Term: 24 to 60 months (2 to 5 years) with fixed weekly or monthly payments; longer terms for larger, longer-lived assets.
- Collateral: the financed equipment only; no additional collateral. A personal guarantee may be required.
- New or used: both qualify; age and condition can affect the amount and term.
- Eligible assets: manufacturing machinery, commercial vehicles and fleets, medical and dental equipment, construction and heavy equipment, restaurant equipment, POS and technology systems.
- Documents: 4 bank statements plus the equipment's type, price and vendor.
- Credit: FICO 500+; because the equipment secures the financing, approval is often more accessible than unsecured financing.
- Versus leasing: with financing you own the equipment at the end; with a lease you pay to use it and may not own it.
- Versus a term loan: equipment financing is secured by the asset and runs 2 to 5 years matched to its useful life; a term loan runs 6 to 48 months and is flexible in purpose.

## Invoice financing
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/invoice-financing/

Invoice financing (accounts receivable financing) turns unpaid invoices from commercial customers into immediate working capital, so you stop waiting 30, 60 or 90 days to get paid.

- Advance: up to 90% of invoice value, depending on invoice quality and your customers' creditworthiness.
- Fees: 0.5% to 5% per month on the advanced amount, plus origination of 0% to 2%. Your specialist confirms the exact rate before you commit.
- Eligible invoices: issued to creditworthy commercial customers (businesses, government entities and organizations) for goods or services already delivered; undisputed and not pledged elsewhere. Consumer invoices are typically not eligible.
- Recourse: you repay the advance even if your customer does not pay.
- Confidential: customers are not notified and keep paying you directly.
- Credit: approval rests mainly on your customers' creditworthiness; SBG's minimum FICO of 500 still applies.
- Versus factoring: with financing you keep ownership of the invoices and collect yourself; factoring sells the invoices to a third party that collects from your customers.

## Bridge capital
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/bridge-capital/

Bridge capital is fast, short-term financing that covers a defined gap until a known repayment event: a closing, an expected payment or permanent financing.

- Amount: $5,000 to $10,000,000.
- Term: a few weeks to several months, tied to the repayment event.
- Collateral: none; bridge capital is unsecured.
- Underwriting: the clarity and certainty of the repayment source is the main factor. You should be able to say when and how the bridge will be repaid before applying.
- Common use: covering a deposit, earnest money or initial costs of a business acquisition while SBA financing closes.
- Documents: 4 bank statements plus evidence of the repayment source (purchase agreement, commitment letter or contract).
- Extensions: evaluated case by case and not guaranteed.

## Business HELOC
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/business-financing/home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc/

A business home equity line of credit lets owners borrow against the equity in their primary residence for business purposes, usually at lower rates than unsecured business financing.

- Credit limit: typically up to 85% of the home's appraised value minus the existing mortgage balance. Example: a $500,000 home with a $250,000 mortgage could support a line of up to $175,000 (85% of $500,000 minus $250,000).
- Rate: usually variable and tied to the prime rate; some HELOCs offer a fixed-rate option on part of the balance.
- Collateral: your home. Draw responsibly and keep a repayment plan, because the home is at risk if the line is not repaid.
- Requirements: a FICO score of 600 or higher, at least 6 months in business and the 4 most recent business bank statements. SBG lists no $150,000 revenue minimum for the HELOC because it is secured by the home.
- Speed: can take longer than SBG's other products.
- If you sell the home, the HELOC balance must be paid off at closing.
- SBG suggests a HELOC when a business does not yet match its other programs but the owner has home equity.
