# Glossary

## Financing terms explained
Source: https://sbgfunding.com/resources/

- Advance: a lump sum provided upfront, as in revenue-based financing, repaid from future sales.
- APR (annual percentage rate): the yearly cost of borrowing including interest and certain fees.
- Collateral: a specific asset pledged to secure financing that the lender can claim if it is not repaid.
- Draw: money taken from a line of credit; interest applies only to drawn amounts.
- Factor rate: a decimal multiplier (for example 1.25) applied to the amount funded to set the total repayment.
- FICO score: the most common personal credit score, from 300 to 850. SBG's minimum is 500 (600 for a HELOC).
- Gross revenue: total sales before expenses; SBG's minimum is $150,000 over the last 12 months.
- Hard credit pull: a credit inquiry that can lower a credit score; SBG does not perform one.
- Soft credit pull: an identity and credit check that does not affect the score; used by SBG when you apply.
- Loan-to-value (LTV): financing as a percentage of an asset's value; equipment financing reaches up to 90% LTV.
- Origination fee: a one-time fee for setting up financing.
- Personal guarantee: the owner's promise to repay if the business cannot.
- Prime rate: the benchmark rate banks charge their best customers; SBA 7(a) and HELOC rates are tied to it.
- Recourse: you remain responsible for repayment even if your customer does not pay an invoice.
- Revolving credit: credit you can draw, repay and draw again, such as a line of credit or HELOC.
- Term: the period over which financing is repaid.
- UCC filing: a public notice that a lender holds a security interest in business assets.
- Working capital: money used for day-to-day operations such as payroll, inventory and rent.
